## Persona 1: Plot-Curious Casey

**The Coward Who Reads the Ending**

> **Quick Profile:** Chronically online 18-34 year old who refuses to watch horror but desperately needs to know what happens in it. Reads Wikipedia plot sections at 1am with the lights on, asks friends to narrate movies, googles "is it scary" before agreeing to anything. Fluent in horror discourse without having seen a single frame.

### Demographics

- **Age:** 18-34 (skews 20s)
- **Location:** Anywhere English-speaking; urban skew
- **Device habits:** Chrome on desktop/laptop for streaming and browsing, TikTok and Instagram daily on mobile
- **Media diet:** Heavy Netflix/YouTube user, active in pop culture conversation, follows movie discourse without watching the movies
- **Social context:** Friend groups and partners who watch horror; regularly the odd one out on movie night

### Psychographics

**Goals:**

- Know the plot, the twist, and the ending of every horror movie people are talking about
- Stay in the group chat conversation without watching anything
- Browse Netflix and YouTube without being ambushed by a Terrifier thumbnail
- Stop pretending they'll "watch it eventually"

**Pain Points:**

- Horror is everywhere: autoplay trailers, thumbnail rows, YouTube recommendations they never asked for
- Wikipedia plot sections are dry and somehow still upsetting
- "Ending explained" articles assume you watched the movie and care about the director's vision
- Friends give terrible summaries: "you just have to see it"
- Mild shame about being scared; tired of "it's not even that scary"

**Motivations:**

- FOMO on cultural moments (everyone is talking about the new A24 thing)
- Control: knowing what happens defuses the fear
- Identity: being a scaredy-cat is funnier as a bit than as a confession
- Comfort: the internet should be a safe place to browse at night

### Behaviors

**Current workflow:**

- Sees a horror title trending → googles "is [movie] scary" or "what happens in [movie]" within minutes *(assumption to validate against Search Console data)*
- Reads the full Wikipedia plot, then the Reddit thread, then still feels unsatisfied
- Asks the horror-fan friend or partner for a verbal summary
- Avoids Netflix's horror rows by scrolling fast and looking slightly away

**Decision process:**

- Installs impulsively from a single funny TikTok or a friend's screenshot; no research phase
- The Chrome Web Store listing has one job: confirm it's free, safe, and as funny as the video that sent them
- Activation moment: first blur plus first "just tell me what happens" click, within minutes of install
- Churns silently if blurs are flaky or the synopses run out

**Information sources:**

- TikTok and Reels (primary discovery)
- Google: "is X scary", "X ending explained", "X jump scare count"
- Group chat screenshots from friends
- Reddit threads they read but never post in

### Jobs-to-be-Done

- **When** everyone is talking about a new horror movie, **I want** the full plot delivered quickly and entertainingly, **so that** I can be part of the conversation without watching it.
- **When** I'm browsing Netflix or YouTube at night, **I want** scary thumbnails and trailers hidden before I see them, **so that** I can relax instead of bracing for ambushes.
- **When** I admit I'm too scared to watch something, **I want** the product to treat that as good taste rather than weakness, **so that** being a scaredy-cat feels like a fun identity instead of an embarrassing one.
- **When** I find a synopsis that made me laugh, **I want** an effortless way to send it to my group chat, **so that** I can be the one who found the thing.

### Key Quote (representative, to be replaced with a real review)

> "I have read the Wikipedia plot of every Conjuring movie and seen none of them. This extension is literally me as a cat."

### Strategic Considerations

**Why this persona first:**

- **Intent is already expressed:** they generate the "is it scary" / "what happens in" search volume the library targets. No demand creation needed.
- **Instant activation:** "just tell me what happens" is built for them; time-to-value is one click.
- **They power the loop:** self-tagging cowardice is shareable, which recruits the partner and anxious-browser segments without targeting them.

**Messaging:**

- Lead with the spoiler payoff, not the blocking: "The cat read it so you don't have to."
- Never frame around anxiety or protection; frame around taste and convenience.
- All copy in the cat's voice; Casey installs because it's funny, stays because it works.

**What would invalidate this persona:**

- Install sources show parents or anxious browsers dominating instead
- "Tell me what happens" click-through on blurs lands under ~30%
- Social comments index on "I need this for my kid" rather than "this is so me"

**Validation plan (first 90 days):** tag install sources, watch which synopses get read versus skipped, read every CWS review and TikTok comment, and rewrite this doc with citations once 20+ real quotes exist.
